  Exploring chess and Go AI paradigms (MCTS, Stockfish NNUE, AlphaZero, alpha-beta search) for Wordle
  is a fascinating direction.
  While 1-step Shannon entropy (your current strategies.py) is greedy, real games require multi-turn
  foresight—especially to avoid "word traps" (e.g. _IGHT or _ATCH, where a greedy 1-step guess leaves
  5 words in a bucket that differ by only 1 letter, forcing you to burn 4 guesses).

  Here are 6 distinct algorithmic paradigms adapted from Game AI and Decision Theory:
  ──────
  ### 1. 🌲 Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) with PUCT (AlphaZero Approach)

  Instead of searching every branch to depth 6, MCTS selectively expands promising branches using
  Upper Confidence Bounds (UCT/PUCT).
  • State S: Defined by (remaining_candidate_set, guess_history).
  • Actions A: The allowed guess list (~14k words).
  • Selection / Expansion: Use PUCT to balance exploration vs exploitation:

                      √(N(S))
    Q(S,a) + c·P(S,a)──────────
                     1 + N(S,a)

  • Prior Policy P(S,a): Fast 1-step entropy or a lightweight neural network ranks the top ~20
  candidate guesses for expansion.
  • Value Evaluation V(S): Roll out using a fast policy or a learned value head that estimates 𝔼[turns
  to solve|S].
  • Why it excels: MCTS detects trap states 2–3 moves ahead. It realizes early that a greedy 1-step
  guess creates a "hard-to-separate" bucket on turn 4, and instead chooses a guess that pre-emptively
  splits the trap.
  ──────
  ### 2. ♟️ Transposition Tables & Dominance Rules (Stockfish Approach)
  Full minimax search is O(Gᵈ·T), but exact/bounded depth search becomes practical when applying
  Stockfish-style optimizations:

  • Transposition Table (TT): Notice that the state in Wordle is strictly defined by the set of
  remaining candidates C. Different guess sequences that yield the exact same candidate pool C reach
  identical states!

    TT [frozenset(C)] → (best\_guess,min\_depth\_to\_solve)

 • Information Bounds (Alpha-Beta Pruning):
      • Lower Bound: |C| = k implies min turns ≥ lceil log₃ k rceil.
      • Dominance Rules: If |C| ≤ 2, the optimal move is trivial (guess word 1, then word 2).
      • Partition Deduplication: If two guess words yield the exact same partition of C, prune one.
  • Iterative Deepening: Evaluate 1-step, then 2-step lookahead, reusing TT bounds to prune 95%+ of
  the tree.
  ──────
  ### 3. 🧠 NNUE / Feature-Based Position Evaluators (Stockfish 12+ / Leela)

  Instead of running deep tree searches during interactive play, train a fast evaluation function V(C)
  that scores the "health" or "entropy quality" of a candidate pool C.

  • Feature Extraction on Candidate Pool C:
      1. Separability Graph: Construct a graph where edges connect candidate words that differ by only
      1 letter. Dense clusters indicate dangerous traps.
      2. Letter Co-occurrence Matrix: Measure letter entropy across unrevealed positions.
      3. Target Frequency Weights: Incorporate word-frequency priors.
  • Value Head V(C): A small MLP or linear model predicts the expected number of turns required to
  solve C.
  • Play Phase: Rank guesses by

    E⎡V⎛C           ⎞⎤
     ⎣ ⎝ after guess⎠⎦

  —1-step lookahead into a 0.1ms neural position evaluator.
  ──────
  ### 4. 🔮 2-Step Lookahead / Information Bottleneck

  Full d-step lookahead is expensive, but 2-step expected information gain is very fast:

    𝔼⎡H ⎛g ⎞⎤ = ∑ P⎛B ⎞·max   H⎛g |B ⎞
     ⎣ 2⎝ 1⎠⎦   k  ⎝ k⎠    g   ⎝ 2  k⎠
                            2

  • Implementation: Compute 1-step entropy H₁ for all 14k guesses. Take the top K = 30 candidate
  guesses for g₁, and for each resulting bucket Bₖ (where |Bₖ| > 2), find the best g₂.
  • Entropy Variance Penalty: Penalize guesses whose bucket size distribution has high variance or
  large "hard-to-resolve" buckets.
  ──────
  ### 5. 🎮 Reinforcement Learning (PPO / Policy Gradient)

  Train an RL agent in a simulated Wordle environment to learn non-myopic policies.

  • Observation Space: 5 × 26 letter constraint matrix (green/yellow/gray statuses) + 5 × 26 remaining
  candidate letter frequency matrix.
  • Reward Function: +10 for solving in ≤6 guesses, -1 per guess, -100 for failing (game over).
  • Learned Strategy: RL agents discover "sacrificial guesses"—for example, intentionally playing a
  non-candidate word with 5 unique consonants (e.g. CLIMB or STOMP) in round 2 purely to eliminate
  letters, even if it yields 0 probability of winning on that turn.
  ──────
  ### 6. ⚖️ Risk-Averse Bayesian Decision Theory (Asymmetric Loss)

  Standard Wordle rewards solving in fewer guesses, but strictly penalizes exceeding 6 guesses.

  • Asymmetric Loss Function:

    Loss (g) = 𝔼[turns] + λ·P(turns > 6)

  • Behavior Shift: When candidates are large or round ≥4, the strategy shifts from expected-value
  maximization to worst-case risk minimization, prioritizing safety over aggressive candidate guesses.
  ──────
